Arpeggiator swing¶

Grid and Euclidean rhythms accept swing, an exact rational first-interval share of a two-step pair. It defaults to "1/2" (straight timing), must be at least one half, and must be less than one. "2/3" gives a 2:1 pair. Custom patterns and source rhythms do not accept this field.

For straight step duration d and share s, pair k begins at 2*k*d. Its second opportunity is at 2*k*d + 2*d*s; the next pair starts at 2*(k+1)*d. The first interval is 2*d*s and the second is 2*d*(1-s). Gate lengths use the interval after the swung start times have been established.

Pair phase follows the absolute local step index, including rests, rejected chance decisions, and empty banks. Euclidean rotation affects its hit mask; it does not reset swing. Odd-length Euclidean loops alternate their swing alignment. Chord edits do not reset rhythm phase. Transport seeks find the first swung opportunity at or after the target beat without replaying earlier opportunities. Swing is preset-only; there is no live swing control port.

Accent and gate patterns¶

An optional body.patterns table supplies independent nonempty lists of exact, nonnegative multipliers: accents and gates. Each omitted list defaults to [1]. Omitting the table preserves the source velocity and ordinary gate.

Lists index the absolute local rhythm step, including rests, rejected chance, empty banks, and ties, and loop independently. Chord edits do not reset phase; seeks use the target rhythm position. Repeats reuse their parent step's values. A zero accent rejects a new start before chance or selection, without cancelling already sounding occurrences. Untied zero-gate hits still create an immediate start/end.

Gates multiply the current global gate after start times are established. Tie lookahead uses the multiplier at each tied cell to find the final release; ties retain the originating accent and never create a new start. Already scheduled repeats and ties keep their realized settings when controls or banks change.

MIDI adapters multiply source attack velocity, round halves upward, and clamp positive results to 1–127. Release velocity, raw source events, and breath, bend, and pressure values are retained. Generic occurrences carry the exact accent multiplier without interpreting the payload. These lists are preset-only.

Conditional hits¶

A custom hit cell may include condition = {passes = [1, 3], out_of = 4}. Passes are numbered from 1 and repeat after out_of complete rhythm-pattern passes. The list must be nonempty, contain unique positive integers, and not exceed the positive integer out_of. Omission admits the hit on every pass. Only hit cells accept conditions.

For absolute cell index i and pattern length n, the current pass is (i // n) % out_of + 1. All cells, silent passes, empty banks, and chord edits retain this rhythm phase. Seeking derives it from the target transport position. A failed condition acts as a rest before probability and selection, consuming neither a chance draw nor a selected note. Accent and gate lists keep advancing. Repeats share the hit's condition result. Admitted hits sustain through following ties, including ties across a pass boundary; skipped hits start no occurrence for those ties to extend. Conditions do not cut off previously scheduled notes.

Finite rhythm passes¶

Custom patterns and Euclidean rhythms may specify cycles = N, a strictly positive integer. Omission loops indefinitely. A custom pass contains every cell in steps, including rests and ties; a Euclidean pass contains its declared number of steps, independent of rotation, pulses, and swing. Plain grids and source rhythms do not accept this setting.

Passes follow absolute transport position and include silent or empty-bank passes. Chord edits do not restart the allowance. Seeking before the ending makes the corresponding passes available; seeking to or beyond it admits no hits. Conditional pass counters retain their existing transport phase.

At the limit no further attacks or cycle-start notifications are generated. The underlying step clock and controls continue. Already scheduled attacks and gates complete normally, including gates beyond the ending. Tie lookahead stops before cells in unplayed passes. Stop, pause, and seek retain their existing cleanup semantics; pausing does not renew the allowance.

Groove templates¶

Grid and Euclidean rhythms may specify groove = [3, 1, 2, 2], a nonempty list of strictly positive exact rational interval weights. The interval at absolute step i is step * len(groove) * groove[i % len(groove)] / sum(groove). Each complete timing template retains step * len(groove) beats. On a quarter beat grid the example intervals are ⅜, ⅛, ¼, ¼ beats. Starts follow those intervals; gates multiply the resulting interval. Authored weights round-trip without normalization in the document.

The template repeats independently of Euclidean pass length, rotation, finite cycle limits, accent/gate lists, and note selection. Rests, rejected hits, empty banks, and chord edits preserve phase. Seeking uses the destination's exact rhythm position. A partial template at a finite ending is not renormalized.

Omitting groove retains existing swing behavior. An explicit groove requires straight swing = "1/2" (the default); non-straight swing with a groove is an error. Weights [2, 1] express the same timing as swing "2/3". Custom/source rhythms do not accept grooves. Groove settings are preset-only.

Weighted transition selection¶

selection = {kind = "transition", weights = [[0, 3, 1], [1, 0, 1], [4, 1, 0]]} selects the next entry from the row for the previously selected entry's current rank. Rows and columns address the expanded traversal, like weighted choice; entries retain source/layer identities. Nonnegative u32 integer weights express relative likelihood; explicit zero forbids an edge. A nonempty table is required. Rows may be empty or unequal in length. Missing rows/columns use missing_weight, a nonnegative u32 value defaulting to 1. An explicit seed is always required.

start = "lowest" emits rank zero initially without consuming a transition draw; start = "random" chooses uniformly with a seeded draw. When the previous entry survives a chord edit, use its new rank. If it disappears, use the opening rule. Bank-edit retrigger overrides preservation. Empty banks, clear, and stop forget the previous entry; random draw counters retain their existing reset semantics.

If all weights for currently available destinations are zero, blocked = "rest" (the default) emits nothing and retains the previous entry and transition draw counter. This can remain silent until a bank edit, retrigger, or reset exposes a path. blocked = "uniform" permits a uniform draw over all available entries, including the previous entry. It deliberately relaxes zero/missing restrictions. The first opening choice is independent of outgoing edge restrictions.

Rhythm masks, conditions, zero accents, empty banks, and rejected chance decisions do not advance transitions. Probability is evaluated before selection, including on a blocked opportunity. Repeats reuse one selected entry. Transition draws use the independent transition random lane. Realization and selection offsets follow selection as usual, without replacing its remembered source identity. The first implementation supports held/latched banks and generic item banks; captured history/phrase selection and file rendering remain unsupported.

Interval envelopes¶

selection = {kind = "interval_envelope", ranges = [{min = 1, max = 1}, {min = -2, max = 2}], boundary = "wrap"} repeats inclusive signed rank-move ranges. Entries refer to the expanded traversal, so this also works for opaque nonmusical payloads. An explicit seed is required. Range endpoints are signed 64-bit integers, min <= max, with at most 2**64 - 1 possible moves per range. The list is nonempty. Ranges are sampled arithmetically without enumerating moves.

The first selection is rank zero and does not apply a range or draw randomness. Later selections use ranges[absolute_step_index % len(ranges)]. Every rhythm opportunity advances this phase, including masks, rests, ties, zero accents, blocked ranges, rejected chance, and empty banks. Repeats share one selection. A surviving previous identity uses its current rank after edits; removal or normal retrigger resets selection to rank zero without resetting rhythmic phase.

boundary = "wrap" (default) draws uniformly among inclusive signed moves, then wraps the destination rank. Different moves may reach the same entry, so wrapping does not deduplicate destinations. boundary = "rest" first intersects the range with moves remaining inside the bank, then draws uniformly. An empty intersection emits a rest while preserving the previous entry; the next rhythm opportunity advances to the next range and can recover.

Draws use the interval_envelope lane and absolute rhythm index. Pause preserves selection while skipping crossed opportunities; seek derives the destination phase and uses the existing selector reset. Snapshots preserve both. Realization and selection offsets retain their existing order. Held/latched and generic item banks are supported; captured history/phrase selection and file rendering are outside the first implementation.

Finite grammar preparation¶

selection = {kind = "grammar", axiom = ["A"], rules = {A = ["A", "B"], B = ["A"]}, terminals = {A = 0, B = 1}, rounds = 3} prepares the index pattern [0, 1, 0, 0, 1]. Each round simultaneously replaces every current symbol once; new symbols wait until the next round. Symbols without rules persist unchanged. Only final symbols need terminal mappings. Empty replacements can erase symbols, but the final pattern must be nonempty and every final symbol must have a rank.

Symbols are nonempty strings, including multi-character and Unicode names. Terminal ranks are nonnegative signed-64-bit integers. rounds defaults to zero and must be an integer from 0 to 32. The axiom and every intermediate/final expansion may contain at most 4,096 symbols. Exceeding a limit rejects preparation without truncating it, even if a later rewrite could shrink the pattern.

boundary = "wrap" is the default; "rest" skips unavailable ranks. Preparation returns an ordinary index pattern with the same boundary. Playback loops that pattern and inherits index-pattern advancement, repeats, chord-edit/retrigger, empty-bank, chance, and snapshot behavior. No seed is required for grammar rules. Ranks address the current expanded bank and preserve source gestures. The first implementation supports held/latched and generic item banks; captured history, phrase, region selection, and file rendering retain their existing restrictions. No grammar rewriting or executable external code runs in playback callbacks.
